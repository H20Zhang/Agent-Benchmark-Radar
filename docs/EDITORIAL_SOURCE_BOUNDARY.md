# Editorial source boundary

Benchmark notes distinguish four kinds of information:

| Kind | Evidence requirement | What the date means |
|---|---|---|
| Benchmark definition and reported experiment | Primary paper, released artifact or official evaluator | The recorded factual-verification date |
| Recorded result | Named track, release, model, tools, budget, metric and source | Verification of that record, not a live-best claim |
| Selection judgment | An editorial inference from the measurement object and limitations | Editorial revision |
| Illustrative task or suggested experiment | Explicitly labeled illustration or proposal | Editorial revision; not a claim of a released task or completed experiment |

An automated structural audit checks presence, synchronization and local references. It is not a semantic quality grade or a factual certification. Rewriting a note does not advance the benchmark's factual-verification timestamp. When primary evidence is unavailable, preserve the uncertainty rather than filling a template with invented details.
